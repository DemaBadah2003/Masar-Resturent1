our story

From a family table to a neighborhood restaurant.

Twenty-six years of the same recipes, the same olive grove, and a lot more chairs.

1998

It started with Sunday lunch.

Long before Zaytoun had a name, it was a table our grandmother set every Sunday — maqluba, musakhan, whatever the season gave us. Neighbors started showing up uninvited, and eventually we stopped pretending it wasn't a restaurant.

We opened officially in 1998, in the same neighborhood, using the same recipes, measured the same way — by hand, by taste, by memory.

the grove

Our olive oil comes from our own trees.

Every autumn the family still travels back to press the season's olives ourselves. It's the oil that finishes almost every dish that leaves our kitchen — on the hummus, in the taboon dough, drizzled over the knafeh syrup.

It's slower than buying it wholesale. We've never found a reason to change it.
